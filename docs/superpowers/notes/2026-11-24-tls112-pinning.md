# TLS-112: pinning and both-sides-at-once, measured (2026-11-23/24)

Follow-up of TLS-111 (`2026-11-21-tls111-cpu-seconds-on-server1.md`, §5 and the owner's answers
in §6: the host now runs the `performance` power profile; no core isolation; "pinning only if
TLS-112's measured arms justify it").

**Outcome: the arms do not justify a gate change yet.** Neither warm-up timing limit was exceeded
in any run; the unpinned arm's two test-universe excursions (defined in §2) have no established cause,
and pinning did not remove the pass-to-pass swings. E-core placement is argued against only for
one pass (seed 1121, pass 2), and the data do not show one cause for both excursions. Next step (§4): record more per side, investigate the slowdown, and run a full
calibration (with the rounds) before deciding.

## 1. The experiment

Harness: `tls112/pinning.py` (next to this note; it loads `scripts/cdo-speed` as a module and uses
its `Server`, perf attach and pressure readers). One binary, master `c6f61f04` built with
`release-fast`, against itself, on the pinned CDO baseline. One run = one cdo-speed **warm-up**
pass pair: two passes, side order flipped, fresh servers per pass, timed calls = the model build
plus the warm prove, explain, digest, query, tests and impact calls. Not like a real cdo-speed run
in three ways: **rounds 1-3 were not run** (they carry the x1.20 limits, §2), there is no
`wait_calm` admission, and it counts 8 perf events instead of cdo-speed's set.

P-core SMT pairs on server1 (`thread_siblings_list`): (0,5) (1,2) (3,4) (6,7) (8,9) (10,11);
threads 12-21 are E/LP-E cores with no sibling.

Arms:
- **unpinned**: cdo-speed's warm-up pass (sides in turn, no affinity).
- **pinned**: the same, both servers' process trees on the 12 P threads (0-11).
- **both**: both servers at once, base on threads 0-5 (cores {0,5} {1,2} {3,4}) and head on 6-11,
  sets swapped in pass 2; each timed call starts on both sides at a barrier after the perf attach
  (start skew at most 5 ms). Its builds run side by side, which TLS-79 ruled out for cdo-speed.

Loads: **alone**; **e-burn** (a busy loop on each of 12-21); **sib-burn** (a busy loop on the
second thread of every P-core, 2,4,5,7,9,11).

3 rounds x 3 arms x 3 loads, arm/load order shuffled per round (seeds 1121-1123), each round in
its own timed CDO-slot hold. 54 passes, none voided by a host suspend, the same output on both
sides of every call. Rows: `/work/.al-sem/tools-notes/tls112/all.jsonl`.

Confounds:
- **Re-runs.** Rounds 2 and 3 each stopped once at the harness's 35 GB MemAvailable floor (host
  memory held elsewhere); 5 of the 27 runs were re-run with the same seed, outside the shuffled
  order, in separate holds 1-2 h later. One of the two unpinned excursions (seed 1122) is such a
  re-run; the other was run 8 of 9 in round 1.
- **Heat.** The package read 87-102 °C before a pass (median 100 °C).
- **Our own load.** The burners count in our container's stall share (up to 37 %, where
  cdo-speed labels a block UNDER LOAD above 15 %).
- **Missing counts.** cdo-speed rejected the perf output of 4 side-calls (events counted only part
  of the time), 3 of them warm-tests; they are left out of the clock figures and shrink the E-share correlation sample.
- **The in-turn arms' untimed rebuild** of the first side is not in `both`.

## 2. Results (master against master: 1.000 is ideal)

**What cdo-speed judges in a warm-up** (its module doc, "What is judged"): only the calls of at
least `WARM_MIN_SECONDS`, the model build and the test universe (`warm-tests`), each on the
geometric mean of its two passes, CPU at most x1.30 and wall at most x1.60. It fails only upward
(head slower). The x1.20 CPU / x1.30 wall limits belong to rounds 1-3 (median of the per-round
ratios, and the round total), which this run does not measure.

Worst upward reading over the 9 runs of each arm (all three loads):

| arm | build CPU | build wall | warm-tests CPU | warm-tests wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| unpinned | x1.041 | x1.046 | x1.201 | x1.424 |
| pinned | x0.996 | x1.062 | x1.047 | x1.082 |
| both | x1.065 | x1.044 | x1.025 | x1.120 |

Neither warm-up timing limit (x1.30 CPU, x1.60 wall) was exceeded. The full gate also checks peak
memory and call success, and can add a flipped pair after a contradicted failure; this run
measured none of that. An **excursion** here is a warm-tests pair geometric mean of CPU above x1.10
(the only readings above x1.06 CPU in the 27 runs are the two below). Both are unpinned alone runs:
x1.201 and x1.156 CPU (limit x1.30), x1.311 and x1.424 wall (limit x1.60). Under e-burn and
sib-burn unpinned showed none.

**Spread per arm / load, warm-tests, 3 pairs each** (`pinning.py --summary`, pairs by seed, round
and pass number; pair geometric mean min-max, then the largest single pass):

**Per pass, warm-tests CPU (alone runs):**

| arm | seed 1121 | seed 1122 | seed 1123 |
|---|---|---|---|
| unpinned | 0.948 / 1.522 | 1.077 / 1.241 | 0.916 / 0.955 |

Pass-to-pass disagreement on warm-tests: unpinned x1.61 CPU, pinned x1.60. This looks like TLS-111 §4's
two-state call cost (the call costs about 45 s or about 60-75 s), but that is not shown. In the
x1.52 pass (seed 1121, pass 2) both sides ran about the same E-core share of instructions (0.47
and 0.52) at the same P clock (3.03 and 2.98 GHz) with equal instructions, and most of the extra
CPU is system time (20.93 of 25.04 s). That argues against a simple placement explanation for that
pass only. Over the 17 unpinned passes with counts, the sides' E-share difference correlates with
the log CPU ratio at only 0.29 (4 side-calls have no counts, which also thins this figure).

The second side is not a clean explanation either. In the unpinned alone rerun (seed 1122) head was
slower in both passes (CPU x1.077 / x1.241, wall x1.470 / x1.379) although it ran first in pass 1,
so that pair did not cancel an order effect; one side in its pass 2 has no perf counts. Seed 1121
does show a second-side CPU penalty in both passes. In both in-turn arms `warm-tests` runs second
on the server that was rebuilt, so call order and server history are confounded.

**`both` against `pinned`:** not told apart with 9 runs each (upward, 2 judged rows each way).
Against `both`: its sides' clocks differ most (head/base up to x1.157, pinned x1.050) and its build
passes disagree most (x1.40 CPU).

**The clock under the `performance` profile** (warm-tests, P-cores, user cycles / ref-cycles x
3.09 GHz; cycles per user second agree within 1 %): alone 2.87-3.52 GHz, e-burn 2.46-2.99,
sib-burn 2.70-3.25. Under e-burn the P clock stayed at or above 2.46 GHz in 34 side-calls
(TLS-111: 1.57-1.88, n = 3, 6 single threads, another day): consistent with the profile change,
not isolated from it. e-burn still lowers the median clock (about 3.0-3.1 to 2.75-2.92 GHz).

## 3. What it means

- **Pinning is not justified by this run.** The contrast is 2 excursions in 9 unpinned runs
  against 0 in 18 pinned, all of it in the 3 alone runs per arm, below every limit, with no
  established cause (pinned alone passes swing as much per pass: x1.246 CPU).
- **The warm-up pass pair is not the whole gate.** Nothing here measures what pinning does to the
  round rows (about one thread each, 1-10 s, three samples), which hold the tighter limits.
- **Both designs would change what the gate measures** (12 or 6 threads instead of 22, so lost
  parallelism shows only up to that), which needs a re-calibration of every limit.

## 4. Next step

No gate change now. Instead:
1. **Record, per side, the E-core share of instructions and the warm-tests state** in
   `speed.json` (the `cpu_core`/`cpu_atom` counts are already taken since TLS-111), riding the ~20
   landings the CLOCK/CPI bands already wait for.
2. **Investigate the slowdown.** The same call on the same tree costing x1.2-1.6 more on one side
   in a pass, pinned or not, is the larger effect in this run. Separate call order from server
   history (rebuilt or not), and look at system time, which carried most of one excursion.
3. **Only then a full master-against-master calibration of the pinned design** (rounds 1-3
   included, at least 20 alone pass pairs, interleaved with unpinned), adopting pinning only if the
   warm-tests tail and the round rows tighten there.

Fable's audit of the first version of this note (which over-claimed on all three points above) is
the source of the corrections.
