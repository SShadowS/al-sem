# TLS-112: pinning and both-sides-at-once, measured (2026-11-23/24)

Follow-up of TLS-111 (`2026-11-21-tls111-cpu-seconds-on-server1.md`, §5 and the owner's answers
in §6: the host now runs the `performance` power profile; no core isolation; "pinning only if
TLS-112's measured arms justify it").

## 1. The experiment

Harness: `tls112/pinning.py` (next to this note; it loads `scripts/cdo-speed` as a module and uses
its `Server`, perf attach and pressure readers). One binary, master `c6f61f04` built with
`release-fast`, against itself, on the pinned CDO baseline. One run = one cdo-speed warm-up pass
pair: two passes, side order flipped, fresh servers per pass, timed calls = the model build plus
the warm prove, explain, digest, query, tests and impact calls (cdo-speed's three per-routine
rounds were not run).

P-core SMT pairs on server1 (`thread_siblings_list`): (0,5) (1,2) (3,4) (6,7) (8,9) (10,11);
threads 12-21 are E/LP-E cores with no sibling.

Arms:
- **unpinned**: today's cdo-speed pass exactly (sides in turn, no affinity).
- **pinned**: the same, both servers' process trees on the 12 P threads (0-11).
- **both**: both servers at once, base on threads 0-5 (cores {0,5} {1,2} {3,4}) and head on 6-11,
  sets swapped in pass 2; each timed call starts on both sides at a barrier after the perf attach
  (start skew at most 1 ms). No untimed rebuild in this arm.

Loads: **alone**; **e-burn** (a busy loop on each of 12-21); **sib-burn** (a busy loop on the
second thread of every P-core, 2,4,5,7,9,11: for unpinned and pinned another tenant inside their
threads, for both one per core on each side alike).

3 rounds x 3 arms x 3 loads, arm/load order shuffled per round (seeds 1121-1123), each round in
its own timed CDO-slot hold. Rounds 2 and 3 each stopped once at the harness's 35 GB
MemAvailable floor (host memory held elsewhere); their missing runs were re-run with the same
seed in short timed holds as memory came back. 54 passes, none voided by a host suspend, the same
output on both sides of every call. Rows: `/work/.al-sem/tools-notes/tls112/all.jsonl`.

## 2. Results (master against master: 1.000 is ideal)

**What cdo-speed judges.** Per run: the median of the warm calls' two-pass geometric means (limit
CPU x1.20, wall x1.30), and the build and the test universe (`warm-tests`) on their own (limit CPU
x1.30, wall x1.60). Worst reading over the 9 runs of each arm (all three loads):

| arm | warm median CPU | warm median wall | build CPU | build wall | warm-tests CPU | warm-tests wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| unpinned | x1.104 | x1.121 | x1.096 | x1.139 | **x1.201** | **x1.424** |
| pinned | x1.066 | x1.083 | x1.085 | x1.234 | x1.059 | x1.135 |
| both | x1.131 | x1.135 | x1.099 | x1.086 | x1.025 | x1.120 |

No arm failed master against itself in these 9 runs. The warm-tests row (the row that failed the
landing in TLS-111) is where the arms differ: unpinned read x1.201 and x1.156 CPU (x1.311 and
x1.424 wall) in two of its three alone runs; both pinned arms stayed within x1.06 CPU.

**Per call** (every timed call's two-pass mean, 63 per arm): beyond x1.20 CPU in 7 of 63 calls
unpinned, 3 pinned, 0 both; p90 x1.185 / x1.149 / x1.097. Most of those excursions are the short
warm calls (1-5 s), which cdo-speed judges only through the median.

**The clock under the `performance` profile** (warm-tests, P-cores, user cycles / ref-cycles x
3.09 GHz): alone 2.87-3.52 GHz, e-burn 2.46-2.99, sib-burn 2.70-3.25, against TLS-111's 2.27-3.57
alone and 1.57-1.88 under e-burn. Load on the E cores no longer drops the P cores below their
2.3 GHz base: cause 1 of TLS-111 is mostly gone under the new profile.

Three of the 108 warm-tests side-calls came back without perf counts (attach missed); they are
left out of the clock figures only.

## 3. What it means

- **Pinning is justified for the test universe.** Placement on E cores is the one cause in TLS-111
  that both pinned arms remove, and the unpinned arm's two alone excursions on warm-tests
  (x1.20/x1.16 CPU, x1.31/x1.42 wall: close to or past the limits, on an unchanged tree) are the
  shape of the failed landing. Neither pinned arm came near.
- **`both` is not better than `pinned` on what is judged.** It is tightest on warm-tests and on
  single calls, but it has the worst warm median (x1.131, one alone run at 0.884), it halves each
  side's threads (6), and the two sides tax each other. With 9 runs per arm the two pinned designs
  are not told apart on the judged figures.
- **Both designs change what the gate measures.** `pinned` runs on 12 threads instead of 22, `both`
  on 6. cdo-speed's build and test-universe rows exist to catch lost parallelism, which they then
  see only up to 12 (or 6) threads; every limit was calibrated on 22.

## 4. Recommendation (owner question)

Pin both sides of cdo-speed's timed blocks to the 12 P threads, in turn (the `pinned` arm), and
re-calibrate the limits on it (master against master, ~20 passes, as TLS-65/76 did). It removes
the measured cause of the test-universe excursions with the smallest change to what is measured;
`both` costs more (6 threads, mutual tax) for no gain on the judged rows. Keep the instruction
counts and TLS-111's CLOCK/CPI labels unchanged.
